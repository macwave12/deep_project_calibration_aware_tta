# Calibration-Aware Test-Time Adaptation for CLIP

`report/figures/` and `results/`.

## 1. Title

**Calibration-Aware Test-Time Adaptation for CLIP**
Gal Machlin (207627811) & Idan Yehuda (207016270) — TAU Deep Learning, final project

*Speaking point:* One line on the question — can we make CLIP's test-time adaptation more
trustworthy, not just more accurate, without any training or labels?

---

## 2. CLIP fails out of distribution

**The collapse numbers**

*Speaking point:* CLIP zero-shot accuracy collapses on out-of-distribution image domains — the
benchmark paper we build on reports 23.7% on EuroSAT and 15.7% on Aircraft. Test-time adaptation
(TTA) exists to recover this gap without retraining or labels, using only the test images
themselves.

---

## 3. TTA fixes accuracy

**TDA: a forward-pass-only feature cache**

*Speaking point:* TDA (CVPR 2024) keeps a small cache of features from confident test images and
reuses it to refine later predictions — no backpropagation, no labels. On our own runs it lifts
mean accuracy from 53.16% (zero-shot) to 56.63% across five datasets.

---

## 4. But it breaks calibration

**5.70% → 9.21% ECE**

*Speaking point:* A 2025 benchmark (arXiv:2506.24000) shows this accuracy gain is not free: TDA's
average expected calibration error rises 5.70% → 9.21% across its suite even as accuracy
improves. A model that's right 80% of the time but reports 95% confidence is worse to trust than
one that's honest about the 80%.

---

## 5. Why: the confidence feedback loop

**Confident predictions get cached, cached predictions get reinforced**

*Speaking point:* TDA's cache is filled by an entropy gate that only measures how *loud* a
prediction is, and its cache logits are added to CLIP's own logits, so agreement between the two
inflates confidence with no check on whether the agreement was correct. Confident-but-wrong
predictions get rewarded exactly like confident-and-right ones.

---

## 6. Change 1 — margin admission

**Admit by top-1/top-2 gap, not entropy**

*Speaking point:* We gate cache admission by the margin between the top two logits instead of
entropy — a more direct decisiveness signal. Measured admission rates span 15.9% (Aircraft, 100
classes) to 81.9% (Pets, 37 classes): the one global threshold is not portable across datasets of
different class count, and we report that as a measured limitation, not a bug to retune away.

---

## 7. Change 2 — probabilistic fusion

**Mix probabilities, don't sum logits**

*Speaking point:* Instead of adding CLIP's and the cache's raw logits, we take a convex mixture
of their softmax probabilities. In practice this backfires the other way from what we expected:
the cache's own logits are comparatively small and flat, so averaging them into CLIP's sharp
distribution at 50/50 caps top-1 confidence well below 1 on *every* sample, correct or not — the
result is severe underconfidence, not overconfidence, and it is measured to be the single
worst-performing component in our ablation (mean ECE 24.54%).

---

## 8. Change 3 — LOO temperature

**Label-free calibration via leave-one-out cache agreement**

*Speaking point:* We estimate the cache's own accuracy without any ground truth, by leave-one-out
nearest-neighbour agreement among cached pseudo-labels, then pick a softmax temperature that
makes mean confidence match that estimate. Because temperature scaling is a monotonic rescaling,
it structurally cannot change which class wins — so this change can only affect calibration,
never accuracy.

---

## 9. Results — the scatter figure

**`analysis/figures/accuracy_vs_ece.png`**

*Speaking point:* All three contributions bundled together, as specified, **fail**: mean ECE
rises from TDA's 11.24% to 24.12%, worse on 4 of 5 datasets, with lower accuracy than TDA on
every single dataset. But isolating each contribution in a 40-run ablation shows temperature
scaling *alone* — margin admission and fusion both off — matches TDA-equivalent accuracy exactly
(56.60%, structurally guaranteed) while cutting mean ECE to 8.54%, the best of all eight ablation
variants. The purple diamond on the scatter is that configuration.

---

## 10. Honest limitations and conclusion

**One idea won, two didn't — and we know why**

*Speaking point:* Margin admission and probabilistic fusion are both actively harmful on
average, and neither is consistently helpful even alone. Temperature scaling alone reverses on
Pets, where TDA was already well calibrated — it always smooths toward its label-free estimate
with no way to detect that smoothing wasn't needed. The finding we stand behind is narrower than
what we set out to build: not "three changes fix TDA's calibration," but "one of three does, run
alone, and we've diagnosed exactly why the other two don't."
