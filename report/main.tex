\title{Agreement-Gated TDA: the cache that echoes CLIP, and a parameter-free fix}
\author{Gal Machlin (207627811) \and Idan Yehuda (207016270)}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
TDA~\cite{karmanov2024tda} adapts CLIP at test time with a cache of past test features labelled by CLIP's own
pseudo-labels, and gains accuracy at the cost of calibration. We trace the calibration loss to a mechanism: the cache
is written before it is read, and is filled with CLIP's own decisions, so on most samples it \emph{echoes} CLIP. We prove
that wherever the cache's top class agrees with the top class of CLIP plus TDA's negative cache, the positive cache
cannot change the decision and can only raise confidence. The fix follows: use the positive cache only when its top
class differs from CLIP's. The \emph{agreement gate} has no parameters and costs one comparison per sample. On five test
sets (CLIP ViT-B/16, \mainSeeds{} stream orders) it keeps TDA's accuracy (\mainGateAcc{} vs \mainTdaAcc{}) and halves its
ECE (\mainGateEce{} vs \mainTdaEce{}). On \genAllN{} further datasets never used in its design it keeps accuracy
everywhere and lowers ECE on \genAllEceWins{} of them; on the fresh tier the mean ECE moves only from
\genFreshMeanTdaEce{} to \genFreshMeanGateEce{}, because where CLIP is underconfident TDA's echo happens to correct it. Across \rhoN{} datasets the gate's
ECE change tends to follow CLIP's own calibration gap (Spearman $\rho = \rhoGapAll$). An ablation by agreement group
confirms that on agreeing samples the positive cache adds no accuracy on any tier, only confidence; and ten label-free
scores of CLIP, of the cache and of their relation, alone or combined, carry almost no information about which of the
remaining flips are right, so finer agreement-based rules do not beat the binary gate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Zero-shot CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip} is reasonably calibrated, but test-time adaptation (TTA) makes it overconfident:
a recent benchmark~\cite{sheng2025illusion} shows that TTA methods, TDA among them, raise accuracy and ECE together.
TDA is the natural object of study: it is training-free, backpropagation-free and fast, and it is the method later
cache-based TTA work builds on. On our five test sets TDA adds \mainTdaOverClipAcc{} accuracy points to CLIP and
\mainTdaOverClipEce{} ECE points (Table~\ref{tab:main}).

\textbf{Contributions.} (1) A \emph{diagnosis}: TDA's positive cache, filled with CLIP's pseudo-labels and read after the
current sample is written, echoes CLIP. We show the echo empirically (the self-vote, \S\ref{sec:diag}) and formally
(Lemmas~\ref{lem:noflip}--\ref{lem:agree}): agreement can only inflate confidence. (2) The \emph{agreement gate}, a
cause-level fix: a parameter-free rule that uses the cache only where it can change the decision. (3) Evidence on
\totalDatasets{} datasets in three tiers (design, development, fresh), with matched and post-hoc baselines, including where
the gate does \emph{not} help (mostly where CLIP is underconfident). (4) Two checks of the limit: an ablation by
agreement group (the agreeing positive cache carries no accuracy) and a flip-information analysis (CLIP's and the
cache's scores carry almost no information about which flips are right).
\textbf{Relation to ZAEC.} ZAEC~\cite{jiang2026zaec} observed empirically that adapted VLMs sharpen predictions they do not
change, and corrects this post hoc with a per-sample entropy-matching temperature. We explain \emph{why} a pseudo-label
cache does this and remove the cause inside the cache; the two act at different points and combine
(Table~\ref{tab:comparison}). The gate is an inference-time instance of the principle ``update only on disagreement''
of \citet{malach2017decoupling}; Lemma~\ref{lem:noflip} is a simple margin bound in the spirit of MARC~\cite{tamim2026marc}.

\section{Background: TDA}
For an image feature $f(x)$, CLIP gives logits $z$ (class-prompt cosines times CLIP's logit scale) and a pseudo-label
$\hat y_0=\arg\max_k z_k$. TDA keeps two caches of at most $S=3$ features per class. The \emph{positive} cache stores
low-entropy samples under $\hat y_0$; the \emph{negative} cache stores mid-entropy samples with a mask of the classes CLIP
finds unlikely. Each sample is first \emph{written} (if admitted) and then \emph{read}. The output logits are
\begin{equation}
o(x) = \underbrace{z(x) - n(x)}_{b(x)} + \alpha\, c(x), \qquad
c_k(x) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}_k} \exp\!\big(-\beta\,(1 - \langle f(x), f_i\rangle)\big) \in [0, S],
\label{eq:tda}
\end{equation}
where $n$ is the negative-cache term and $\mathcal C_k$ the positive cache of class $k$. We use one setting for every
dataset, $\alpha = 2$, $\beta = 5$ (``P2''); the published per-dataset settings (``P1'') are in Appendix~\ref{app:p1}
and change none of the conclusions.

\section{Diagnosis: the cache echoes CLIP}\label{sec:diag}
All diagnosis is on the val splits of the five core datasets, where the method was designed; test numbers appear only
in \S\ref{sec:results}.

\textbf{The self-vote.} Because a sample is written before it is read, an admitted sample finds itself in the cache:
$\langle f(x), f(x)\rangle = 1$ adds exactly $1$ to $c_{\hat y_0}(x)$, while every other cached item adds less than $1$.
This term carries no information about the true label. It is frequent (\valSelfMin--\valSelfMax\% of the stream) and
almost decides agreement: the cache's top class equals CLIP's for \valAgreeSelfMin--\valAgreeSelfMax\% of self-voting
samples against \valAgreeNoSelfMin--\valAgreeNoSelfMax\% of the others (Fig.~\ref{fig:diag}a). Removing only the self
term lowers TDA's val ECE from \valTdaEce{} to \valNoSelfEce{} (Fig.~\ref{fig:diag}b; accuracy \valTdaAcc{} $\to$
\valNoSelfAcc{}). The self-vote is the most visible case of a broader echo, which the next two results make precise.

\begin{lemma}[no-flip region]\label{lem:noflip}
If $z_{\hat y_0}(x) - z_j(x) > \alpha S + \max_k n_k(x) - \min_k n_k(x)$ for all $j \neq \hat y_0$, then
$\arg\max o(x) = \hat y_0$ whatever the cache holds.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[agreement]\label{lem:agree}
Let $\hat y = \arg\max_k b_k(x)$. If also $\hat y = \arg\max_k c_k(x)$, then $\arg\max o(x) = \hat y$, every margin
$o_{\hat y} - o_j$ is at least $b_{\hat y} - b_j$, and $\max \mathrm{softmax}(o) \ge \max \mathrm{softmax}(b)$.
\end{lemma}
\noindent\emph{Proof.} $o_{\hat y} - o_j = (b_{\hat y} - b_j) + \alpha(c_{\hat y} - c_j) \ge b_{\hat y} - b_j > 0$, and the
top softmax probability $1/\sum_j e^{-(o_{\hat y}-o_j)}$ is increasing in every margin. Lemma~\ref{lem:noflip} follows the same
way from $0 \le c_k \le S$. Full statements and proofs: Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}. $\square$

\textbf{From the lemma to the data.} The gate (\S\ref{sec:method}) tests the observable condition
$\arg\max c = \hat y_0$ (CLIP's top class), while Lemma~\ref{lem:agree} needs $\arg\max c = \arg\max b$. The two differ
only where the negative cache has moved CLIP's top class, which on val is \valAgreeNegMoved\% of the agreeing samples;
TDA changes the decision on \valAgreeTdaFlips\% of them, with no net accuracy gain (below). So on the agreeing samples
the positive cache mostly adds confidence, and its accuracy gain comes from the disagreeing ones.

The data show how large the inflation is (Fig.~\ref{fig:diag}c,d). On val, the cache agrees with CLIP on
\valAgreeShare\% of samples. There, the positive cache moves accuracy from \valAgreeBaseAcc{} to \valAgreeTdaAcc{} but the
confidence$-$accuracy gap from \valAgreeBaseGap{} to \valAgreeTdaGap{} points. On disagreeing samples it carries the gain
(\valDisagreeBaseAcc{} $\to$ \valDisagreeTdaAcc{}) at a small change of gap (\valDisagreeBaseGap{} $\to$
\valDisagreeTdaGap{}). How much confidence the echo adds is not tied to how calibrated CLIP was, so on most datasets it
overshoots; where CLIP is underconfident on the agreeing samples (Pets), the echo happens to help.

\section{Method: the agreement gate}\label{sec:method}
Use the positive cache only where it can change the decision:
\begin{equation}
o(x) = b(x) + \alpha(x)\,c(x), \qquad \alpha(x) = \alpha \cdot \mathbf{1}\big[\arg\max_k c_k(x) \neq \hat y_0(x)\big].
\end{equation}
Caches, admission and the negative cache are TDA's, unchanged. The rule compares with CLIP's pseudo-label $\hat y_0$, the
label the cache is filled with; it has no parameter, needs no label, and costs one $\arg\max$ comparison. It was fixed
on val before any test, development or fresh result of the gate was computed.

\textbf{Where it helps} (Fig.~\ref{fig:res}b). On agreeing samples the echo only adds confidence
(Lemma~\ref{lem:agree}), so removing it should help when CLIP is calibrated or overconfident and hurt when CLIP is
underconfident. Across all \rhoN{} datasets the gate's $\Delta$ECE tends to follow CLIP's own confidence$-$accuracy gap
(Spearman $\rho = \rhoGapAll$; \rhoGapSeen{} on test + dev, \rhoGapFresh{} on the fresh sets). The relation is a tendency,
not a rule: GTSRB (CLIP slightly overconfident) and Food101 lose a little, and VOC2007, Cars and ImageNet gain although

proxies on the first \signalHead{} stream samples are weaker ($\rho = \rhoConfAll$ for CLIP's mean confidence,
\rhoOverAll{} for the share above \confCut, \rhoShareAll{} for the no-flip share): confidence is not calibration
(CIFAR-100 is unconfident \emph{and} underconfident). The gate is reported always on, with this as its stated condition.

\subsection*{Is there information beyond binary agreement?}\label{sec:noinfo}
The gate uses one bit: does the cache's top class agree with CLIP's. Could a finer, still label-free, use of the scores
do better? Its accuracy gain comes from \emph{flips}, samples where TDA's decision differs from CLIP's; a flip is right
if TDA is right and wrong if CLIP was. We score ten label-free per-sample quantities of CLIP's scores (confidence, margin,
probability of the cache's class), of the cache's scores (confidence, mass, weight on CLIP's class) and of their
relation (correlation over classes, JS divergence, weight gap, the $\alpha$ needed to flip) by how well they separate
right from wrong flips (Fig.~\ref{fig:noinfo}). On the development and fresh sets every single quantity has an AUC
between \niHeldAucLo{} and \niHeldAucHi, close to chance, and the ones that look informative on val and test reverse on
the fresh tier. All ten combined in a logistic regression fitted on the val flips reach AUC \niCombAucVal{} on val and
\niCombAucTest{} on test (the same five datasets) but only \niCombAucDev{} on dev and \niCombAucFresh{} on fresh: what it
learns is which datasets flip well, not which flips are right. Used as a flip filter (revert to CLIP when the predicted
probability of a right flip is below \niThresh, chosen on val) it changes accuracy by at most \niFilterMax{} points on
any tier, whereas blocking every wrong flip would add \niOracleBlockTest{} (test) to \niOracleBlockFresh{} (fresh)
points and picking the right one of CLIP's and the cache's top class \niOraclePickLo--\niOraclePickHi. The headroom
exists, but CLIP's scores, the cache's scores and their relation carry almost no information about it. Within
agreement-based control of the cache, the binary gate is close to the limit; the rates by score are in
Appendix~\ref{app:switch}.

\section{Limitations}
(i) \emph{Underconfident CLIP.} Where CLIP is underconfident (Pets, Caltech101, CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-R, STL-10) the echo happens to
correct it and the gate is worse than TDA; GTSRB and Food101 also lose slightly without that explanation. The exploratory
confidence switch recovers the confident ones but rests on about five datasets and cannot see CIFAR-100 or ImageNet-R;
other uses of the agreement signal did not help (Appendix~\ref{app:switch}).
(ii) The gated branch is still overconfident on the hardest sets (e.g.\ DTD); ZAEC on top helps on the test sets but not
on average on the development and fresh tiers. (iii) One backbone
(ViT-B/16) and one cache method; the echo argument applies to any cache filled with the model's own pseudo-labels
(e.g.\ DMN), which we did not test. (iv) The gate was chosen on the val splits of the five core datasets after earlier
attempts, some explored on test (Appendix~\ref{app:earlier}); the development and fresh tiers are the clean evidence.

\section{Conclusion}
TDA's positive cache echoes CLIP: where it agrees with CLIP, it cannot change a decision and only adds confidence. Gating
it on disagreement keeps TDA's accuracy everywhere and halves TDA's ECE on the design-tier test sets and on ImageNet. On
the fresh tier the gain is smaller on average and not uniform (large on ImageNet-A and -Sketch, losses elsewhere), mostly because CLIP is underconfident on several of those
sets; removing the echo is the right move when CLIP is not underconfident. Agreement is useful as one bit: the agreeing
positive cache adds no accuracy, and the label-free scores of CLIP and the cache say almost nothing about which of the
other flips are right, so the remaining accuracy headroom needs information they do not carry. Keeping the echo on
confident streams is a promising,
not yet clean, extension; detecting underconfidence without labels is the open problem.

\section{Proofs}\label{app:proofs}
\textbf{Lemma 1.} Write $b = z - n$ and $\hat y_0 = \arg\max z$. For $j \ne \hat y_0$,
$b_{\hat y_0} - b_j = (z_{\hat y_0} - z_j) - (n_{\hat y_0} - n_j) \ge (z_{\hat y_0} - z_j) - (\max n - \min n) > \alpha S$.
Since $0 \le c_k \le S$, $o_{\hat y_0} - o_j = (b_{\hat y_0} - b_j) + \alpha(c_{\hat y_0} - c_j) > \alpha S - \alpha S = 0$. $\square$

\textbf{Lemma 2.} As in the main text; strictness of $b_{\hat y} - b_j > 0$ holds for the unique $\arg\max$. The top softmax
probability is $\big(1 + \sum_{j \ne \hat y} e^{-(o_{\hat y} - o_j)}\big)^{-1}$, increasing in each margin, and each margin
of $o$ is at least the corresponding margin of $b$. $\square$

\textbf{The self-vote bound.} If $x$ was admitted under $\hat y_0$ before the read, $c_{\hat y_0}(x) \ge e^{0} = 1$, and each
of the other at most $S$ terms of any class is at most $1$. A disagreement needs another class with kernel mass above
$c_{\hat y_0}(x) \ge 1$, i.e.\ at least two close neighbours of one other class.

\section{Exploratory: a confidence switch and other uses of agreement}\label{app:switch}
\textbf{Rule.} Keep TDA's logits while the running mean of CLIP's max-softmax confidence over the samples seen so far
exceeds a threshold; use the gate otherwise. It is online and label-free. \textbf{Selection.} The threshold is chosen on
the design tiers only, the val splits of the five original sets and the four development sets ($^\ast$ in
Table~\ref{tab:switch}), which contain both kinds of stream: three confident sets on which CLIP is underconfident and six
unconfident ones on which it is overconfident (Table~\ref{tab:switchdesign}). It is the centre of the design plateau (all
thresholds within 0.1 ECE of the design minimum, [\swPlateauLo, \swPlateauHi]), giving \swThresh. No design set has a
mean confidence inside the plateau, so any value in it gives the same design result: the threshold is underdetermined
rather than fitted. The test splits and the fresh sets are held out. The fresh tier alone would have preferred a lower
threshold (\swFreshBestLo--\swFreshBestHi, ECE \swFreshBestEce), so the chosen value is not tuned to it.

\textbf{Reading.} The switch is effectively a dataset-level choice: on the dev, test and fresh tiers it is on for almost the whole
stream of \swOnNames{} (final mean confidence $\ge$ \swMinOnConf) and off elsewhere ($\le$ \swMaxOffConf). Those sets carry
the whole gain; the others are unchanged. It does not reach CIFAR-100 or ImageNet-R, where CLIP is underconfident but not
confident. On the fresh sets it beats the gate clearly but plain TDA only narrowly, and only \swFreshOnN{} of them switch
on.
\textbf{Caveat:} the idea came after the per-dataset fresh results were known, and a similar dataset-level switch had been
dropped before the fresh sets were evaluated, so the held-out tiers are not clean evidence for it; it needs new datasets
to be confirmed.

\textbf{What did not help} (exploratory, parameters chosen on val, checked on test, dev and fresh): $\alpha$ graded by the
strength of disagreement (it reduces to a rescaled constant $\alpha$); larger $\alpha$ (accuracy peaks at $\alpha \approx 2$--3
and the gated branch grows more overconfident); softening the disagreeing branch by the CLIP--cache conflict (helps on test,
hurts on dev and fresh); the cache--CLIP agreement rate as a label-free accuracy estimate (worse than CLIP's own mean
confidence, since the cache votes with CLIP's pseudo-labels); and flip rules on disagreeing samples in terms of the $\alpha$
needed to flip and CLIP's confidence (no held-out accuracy gain, although picking the right of CLIP and the cache per
sample would add several points); and $\alpha$ scaled by the per-sample correlation between the cache and CLIP logits
over classes, including a bump that peaks at intermediate correlation and vanishes at both ends (no held-out accuracy
gain; the bump's val gain reverses on test and fresh, and the variants that lower ECE do so by flipping fewer samples).
These failures follow from \S\ref{sec:noinfo}: Fig.~\ref{fig:rates} shows the share of right flips along each label-free
quantity. Where a curve slopes on val and test it is flat or reversed on the fresh tier; the one quantity with the same
direction everywhere, the $\alpha$ needed to flip, leaves even its worst group of flips right \niAlphaMinRate\% of the
time, so blocking flips by it cannot raise accuracy.

\section{Selection history}
Per-dataset settings and the earlier attempts were explored on test before the clean protocol was adopted; the gate was
then designed on val only; a dataset-level switch (``use TDA when CLIP is confident'') was considered, pre-registered
and dropped in favour of the always-on gate before the fresh sets were evaluated. The confidence switch of
Appendix~\ref{app:switch} was explored afterwards.
