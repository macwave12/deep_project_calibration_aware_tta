\begin{abstract}
Test-time adaptation (TTA) methods such as TDA~\cite{karmanov2024tda} use only forward
passes and a feature cache to recover much of the accuracy CLIP loses out of distribution, but
a 2025 benchmark~\cite{sheng2025illusion} showed that the gain comes with a hidden cost: TDA's
cache rewards confident predictions whether or not they are correct, so its accuracy gain arrives
bundled with worse calibration (the benchmark reports average ECE rising 5.70\%
$\rightarrow$ 9.21\% across its suite). We propose three training-free, label-free changes to
TDA's cache loop -- margin-based cache admission, probabilistic fusion, and leave-one-out (LOO)
temperature scaling -- and measure each one on five datasets (\texttt{ViT-B/16}, test split
only) against a TDA-equivalent baseline. Margin admission fails and is dropped: its fixed
threshold starves the cache on hard datasets and biases it toward easy classes. Fusion fails as
proposed, because a nearly flat cache distribution averaged into CLIP's peaked one caps every
prediction's confidence near one half; but \emph{gating} the fusion to samples where CLIP is
torn between two classes, and leaving the cache out elsewhere, repairs it. Gated fusion alone
lowers mean ECE from TDA's 11.24\% to 4.61\%, below zero-shot CLIP's own 5.57\%, at a mean
accuracy of 55.01\% against TDA's 56.63\% and CLIP's 53.16\%. LOO temperature scaling helps on
its own (mean ECE 8.54\% at unchanged accuracy) but does not stack on the gate, since both
remove the same overconfidence. The method we recommend is TDA's cache with gated fusion:
one extra comparison per sample, no labels, no training.
\end{abstract}
