\title{Agreement-Gated TDA: the cache that echoes CLIP, and a parameter-free fix}
\author{Gal Machlin (207627811) \and Idan Yehuda (207016270)}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
CLIP can classify images zero-shot, over labels it was never trained on, but its accuracy degrades on data unlike its
pre-training data.
TDA~\cite{karmanov2024tda} adapts CLIP at test time with a cache of past test features labelled by CLIP's own
predictions; it raises accuracy but makes CLIP overconfident. We argue that most of this calibration loss is an
\emph{echo}: the cache is filled with CLIP's decisions, so on most samples it repeats CLIP's vote, which cannot change the
decision and only adds confidence. The fix follows: use the cache only where its top class differs from CLIP's. This
\emph{agreement gate} has no parameters and costs one comparison per sample. On five test sets (CLIP ViT-B/16), TDA
changes CLIP's accuracy by \mainTdaOverClipAcc{} points and its expected calibration error (ECE) by
\mainTdaOverClipEce{}; the gate keeps the accuracy gain (\mainGateOverClipAcc{}) with ECE at CLIP's level
(\mainGateOverClipEce{}). On held-out datasets far from CLIP's training images, where TDA's accuracy gain is
largest, the gate keeps the gain and removes TDA's calibration loss; on held-out datasets close to them, TDA
changes little and neither does the gate. It helps where CLIP is overconfident and loses where CLIP is underconfident,
since there the echo happens to correct CLIP. Beyond this one bit of agreement, the scores of CLIP and of
the cache carry almost no information about when the cache should be trusted.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\textbf{CLIP and distribution shift.} CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip} embeds images and text in one space. It classifies an
image zero-shot by comparing its feature with the features of class prompts (``a photo of a \{class\}''), with no
training on the target task. Its accuracy, however, depends on how close the test images are to its pre-training data:
on fine-grained or specialised datasets (aircraft models, textures, satellite images) zero-shot CLIP reaches only
\mainClipAcc\% on average over our five core datasets (Table~\ref{tab:main}).

\textbf{Test-time adaptation.} Test-time adaptation (TTA) improves a model on the test distribution while it is being
tested, using only the unlabelled test inputs. Two quantities measure it. \emph{Accuracy} is the share of correct
predictions. \emph{Calibration} asks whether the model's confidence, its top softmax probability $\hat p(x)$, matches how
often it is right. We measure it with the expected calibration error~\cite{guo2017calibration}: the $N$ test samples are
split into $M=20$ equal-width confidence bins $B_1,\dots,B_M$, and
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ECE} = \sum_{m=1}^{M} \frac{|B_m|}{N}\,\big|\mathrm{acc}(B_m) - \mathrm{conf}(B_m)\big|,
\label{eq:ece}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{acc}(B_m)$ and $\mathrm{conf}(B_m)$ are the accuracy and the mean confidence in bin $m$ (reported in \%).
We also use the signed \emph{confidence$-$accuracy gap}, mean confidence minus accuracy: positive means the model is
\emph{overconfident}, negative \emph{underconfident}. ECE is unsigned and weighs both alike, but they are not equally
harmful: an underconfident model defers some predictions it could have kept, while an overconfident one acts on wrong
predictions it trusts. A low ECE matters whenever the confidence is acted on, for example
to defer uncertain samples to a human or to another model.

\textbf{Online zero-shot TTA.} Methods differ in what they assume. Episodic methods such as TPT~\cite{shu2022tpt} and its
calibrated variant C-TPT~\cite{yoon2024ctpt} tune CLIP's prompt on each test image by backpropagation, which is slow,
and forget everything between images. \emph{Online} methods instead see the test set as a stream, one sample at a time,
and accumulate what they learn along it; \emph{zero-shot} methods use no labelled target data at all. Among these, the
cache-based methods are training-free and backpropagation-free: TDA~\cite{karmanov2024tda}, DMN~\cite{zhang2024dmn} and
ECALP~\cite{li2025ecalp}.

\textbf{TDA.} TDA (Fig.~\ref{fig:tda}) keeps a small \emph{positive cache} of past test features, each labelled with the
class CLIP predicted for it (its \emph{pseudo-label}), and a \emph{negative cache} of uncertain samples with the classes
CLIP ruled out. A new sample is compared with the cached features, and the votes of similar cached samples are added to
CLIP's prediction. TDA is fast, needs no training, and later cache methods build on it. It also raises accuracy and ECE
together: a recent benchmark~\cite{sheng2025illusion} reports this for TTA methods in general, and on our five test sets
TDA adds \mainTdaOverClipAcc{} accuracy points to CLIP and \mainTdaOverClipEce{} ECE points (Table~\ref{tab:main}). This
paper is a direct continuation of TDA: it asks where TDA's calibration loss comes from and removes it at its source.

\textbf{Contributions.} (1) A \emph{diagnosis}: TDA's positive cache, filled with CLIP's pseudo-labels, mostly
\emph{echoes} CLIP. Where it agrees with the decision it cannot change it and can only raise confidence; we show this
formally and on data (\S\ref{sec:motivation}). (2) The \emph{agreement gate}, a parameter-free fix that uses the cache only
where it can change the decision (\S\ref{sec:method}). (3) Evidence on \totalDatasets{} datasets, including \genAllN{}
never used in the design, against TDA, post-hoc calibration and other cache methods, with the cases where the gate does
not help (\S\ref{sec:results}). (4) Two checks of the limit: on agreeing samples the cache adds no accuracy, and finer
label-free scores do not tell when to trust it (\S\ref{sec:limits}).

\section{Background}\label{sec:background}
\textbf{CLIP.} For an image $x$ with normalised feature $f(x)$, CLIP's logits over the $K$ classes are
$z_k(x) = \tau \langle f(x), t_k \rangle$, where $t_k$ is the normalised text feature of class $k$'s prompt and $\tau$
CLIP's logit scale. Its prediction, which TDA uses as the pseudo-label, is $\hat y_0(x) = \arg\max_k z_k(x)$; its
confidence is the top probability of $\mathrm{softmax}(z(x))$.

\textbf{TDA.} The test samples arrive as a stream. TDA keeps, for each class $k$, a positive cache $\mathcal C_k$ of at
most $S=3$ features of past samples pseudo-labelled $k$: a sample is \emph{admitted} if its class has room or if its
prediction entropy is lower than that of the class's most uncertain cached sample, which it then replaces. Each sample is
first \emph{written} into the caches (if admitted) and then \emph{read}. The read gives, for every class $k$, the kernel
mass of the class's cached features near $x$:
\begin{equation}
c_k(x) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{C}_k} \exp\!\big(-\beta\,(1 - \langle f(x), f_i\rangle)\big) \in [0, S],
\qquad c(x) = \big(c_1(x), \dots, c_K(x)\big),
\label{eq:cache}
\end{equation}
where $\beta$ sets how fast the weight of a cached sample falls with its distance to $x$. The vector $c(x)$ is the
positive cache's own prediction: $\arg\max_k c_k(x)$ is the class whose cached samples lie closest to $x$, the
\emph{cache's top class}. The negative cache works the same way on samples of medium entropy, storing for each a binary
mask over classes (TDA's negative pseudo-labels, from thresholding CLIP's probabilities at $0.03$); its kernel term
$n(x)$ is subtracted. TDA's output logits are
\begin{equation}
o(x) = b(x) + \alpha\, c(x), \qquad b(x) = z(x) - n(x),
\label{eq:tda}
\end{equation}
where $b$ is CLIP plus the negative cache, and $\alpha$ weighs the positive cache. TDA's paper searches its
hyperparameters once, on the ImageNet validation set, and applies them unchanged to every other dataset: $\alpha = 2$,
$\beta = 5$. We use this setting everywhere (``P2''). TDA's code release instead ships per-dataset values (``P1''); they
change none of the conclusions (Appendix~\ref{app:p1}).

\textbf{The calibration cost, and post-hoc fixes.} TDA's gain in accuracy comes with a large rise in ECE: on our test
sets CLIP's ECE is \mainClipEce{} and TDA's \mainTdaEce{}. This matters beyond the metric:
an overconfident adapted model cannot be trusted to flag its own mistakes, and TDA itself admits samples to its cache by
their confidence. The standard remedy is post-hoc: rescale the logits by a temperature. A global temperature fitted on
labelled data~\cite{guo2017calibration} needs labels, which TTA does not have. ZAEC~\cite{jiang2026zaec} is label-free: it
observed that adapted vision--language models sharpen predictions they do not change, and softens each output to the
uncertainty of the zero-shot model. Per sample, it divides the adapted logits by the smallest temperature $T(x) \ge 1$
for which
\begin{equation}
H\big(\mathrm{softmax}(o(x)/T(x))\big) \ge H\big(\mathrm{softmax}(z(x))\big),
\label{eq:zaec}
\end{equation}
with $H$ the entropy. Both treat the symptom after the fact. We ask why a pseudo-label cache sharpens predictions it does
not change, and remove the cause inside the cache.

\section{Motivation: the cache echoes CLIP}\label{sec:motivation}
Our thesis is that most of TDA's ECE loss comes from \emph{echoing}: the positive cache is filled with CLIP's own
decisions, so it mostly votes for the class CLIP already chose, and adding that vote raises confidence without adding
information. We start with the most visible case, the self-vote, and then show the general mechanism. All the analysis in
this section is on the validation splits of the five core datasets (DTD, Flowers, EuroSAT, Aircraft, Pets), where the
method was designed; test numbers appear only in \S\ref{sec:results}.

\textbf{The self-vote.} Because a sample is written before it is read, an admitted sample finds itself in the cache:
$\langle f(x), f(x)\rangle = 1$ adds exactly $1$ to $c_{\hat y_0}(x)$, while every other cached sample adds less than
$1$. This term carries no information about the true label. It is frequent: \valSelfMin--\valSelfMax\% of the stream
votes for itself. And it almost decides the cache's top class: for self-voting samples the cache's top class equals
CLIP's \valAgreeSelfMin--\valAgreeSelfMax\% of the time, against \valAgreeNoSelfMin--\valAgreeNoSelfMax\% for the others
(Fig.~\ref{fig:diag}a). Removing only this term, by reading the cache before writing (``read-first TDA''), lowers TDA's
val ECE from \valTdaEce{} to \valNoSelfEce{} (Fig.~\ref{fig:diag}b) at a small accuracy cost (\valTdaAcc{} $\to$
\valNoSelfAcc).

\textbf{Agreement in general.} The self-vote is one way for the cache to agree with CLIP; cached neighbours that CLIP
labelled with the same class are another. Call a sample \emph{agreeing} if the cache's top class equals CLIP's,
$\arg\max_k c_k(x) = \hat y_0(x)$, and \emph{disagreeing} otherwise. On agreeing samples, adding the cache cannot help the
decision:

\begin{lemma}[agreement]\label{lem:agree}
Let $\hat y = \arg\max_k b_k(x)$. If also $\hat y = \arg\max_k c_k(x)$, then $\arg\max_k o_k(x) = \hat y$, every margin
$o_{\hat y} - o_j$ is at least $b_{\hat y} - b_j$, and $\max \mathrm{softmax}(o) \ge \max \mathrm{softmax}(b)$.
\end{lemma}
\noindent\emph{Proof.} $o_{\hat y} - o_j = (b_{\hat y} - b_j) + \alpha(c_{\hat y} - c_j) \ge b_{\hat y} - b_j > 0$, and the
top softmax probability $\big(1 + \sum_{j \ne \hat y} e^{-(o_{\hat y}-o_j)}\big)^{-1}$ is increasing in every margin.
$\square$

The lemma is stated for the top class of $b$; CLIP's top class $\hat y_0$ differs from it only where the negative cache
moved the decision, on \valAgreeNegMoved\% of the agreeing val samples. The data confirm the lemma's picture
(Fig.~\ref{fig:diag}c,d; all numbers below are means over the five val sets). On val, \valAgreeShare\% of samples agree.
On them the positive cache leaves accuracy unchanged
(\valAgreeBaseAcc{} $\to$ \valAgreeTdaAcc) but turns a slight underconfidence into strong overconfidence (gap
\valAgreeBaseGap{} $\to$ \valAgreeTdaGap{} points). All of its accuracy gain comes from the disagreeing samples
(\valDisagreeBaseAcc{} $\to$ \valDisagreeTdaAcc). How much confidence the echo adds is not tied to how calibrated CLIP
was, so it usually overshoots; only where CLIP is underconfident on the agreeing samples (Pets) does it happen to help.
The cache is useful exactly where it disagrees.

\section{Method: the agreement gate}\label{sec:method}
Use the positive cache only where it can change the decision, that is, where its top class differs from CLIP's:
\begin{equation}
o(x) = b(x) + \alpha(x)\,c(x), \qquad \alpha(x) = \alpha \cdot \mathbf{1}\big[\arg\max_k c_k(x) \neq \hat y_0(x)\big].
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
The gate compares two predictions, the cache's ($\arg\max_k c_k$) and CLIP's ($\hat y_0$), not the size of any score:
Lemma~\ref{lem:agree} depends only on which class wins in each. It compares with $\hat y_0$ rather than with
$\arg\max_k b_k$ because $\hat y_0$ is the label the cache is filled with, so agreement with it is exactly the echo; the
two choices differ on few samples. Caches, admission and the negative cache are TDA's, unchanged. The rule has no
parameter, needs no label and costs one $\arg\max$ comparison per sample. The idea has a training-time precedent:
\citet{malach2017decoupling} train two networks on noisy labels and update them only on samples where their predictions
disagree, since agreement carries little new information; the gate applies the same idea at inference time, to
combining CLIP with its cache. It was fixed on val before any test or held-out result of the gate was
computed.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
\textbf{Setup.} CLIP ViT-B/16 with the CoOp splits~\cite{zhou2022coop}. The datasets form three tiers. The \emph{design}
tier is the five core datasets: their val splits were used for the motivation and the design, and their test splits give
the main results. The two held-out tiers were never used for design, and are defined by what their images are.
\emph{Near} sets are ordinary photographs of everyday objects, one named by the label, as in the web
image--caption pairs CLIP was trained on: \nearNames{} (a 10k subset of Food101).
\emph{Far} sets are images that are not ordinary photographs of the named object (medical scans, digits, rendered text,
renditions and sketches, facial expressions), or whose labels are finer than web captions name (43 types of traffic
sign): \farNames{} (3000 random test images of FER2013, PCam, SVHN and MNIST; all of Rendered SST2). ImageNet-R and
ImageNet-Sketch use ImageNet's classes, but their images are renditions and sketches, not the photos TDA was tuned on. A far set also needs zero-shot CLIP
above chance, so that its pseudo-labels carry some signal to adapt from. The tiers were revised once after a first look at
the results, and we state the changes as such: VOC2007 left the near tier, because its photos are multi-object scenes
reduced to one label, and Cars (car photos named by make and model) joined it; in the far tier, Country211 (the country
a photo was taken in, which is not a property of the depicted object) was replaced by ImageNet-R and ImageNet-Sketch,
and CLEVR-count, DMLab and Retinopathy left it, because CLIP is at or below chance on them. These and the held-out sets in between (32-pixel thumbnails, ImageNet-derived photos, video
frames of actions) are reported in Appendix~\ref{app:perds}.
ImageNet and ImageNetV2 are left out entirely, because TDA searched its hyperparameters on ImageNet.
TDA and the gate are online, so their output depends on the order in which the test samples arrive: the cache
holds different samples at each point of the stream. We therefore run every method on the same several random orders of
each test set (seeds) and report the mean over orders; differences between methods are paired, computed per seed on the
same order. We extract CLIP features once and replay TDA's stream exactly on CPU from
them (Appendix~\ref{app:p1}); for the analyses of \S\ref{sec:abl} and \S\ref{sec:limits} we log TDA's per-sample terms
$b(x)$ and $c(x)$ and re-evaluate variants on them exactly. Where we give an interval for a paired difference, it is
a 90\% confidence interval for its mean over seeds from Student's $t$ distribution: with 10 seeds, an interval that
excludes zero means the difference is not an artefact of the stream order.

(\gapLoseUnderNames), on which TDA's extra confidence happens to land closer to the accuracy, and GTSRB, on which TDA is
overconfident while the gate's mean confidence matches its accuracy. Where the gate loses in ECE, it errs on the safe
side.

\subsection{Ablation: the agreeing positive cache adds no accuracy}\label{sec:abl}
Does the motivation's picture hold beyond val? We add TDA's positive cache to $b$ (CLIP + negative cache) on the agreeing
samples only, on the disagreeing samples only (= the gate), or on all samples (= TDA), on every tier
(Table~\ref{tab:abl}). On the agreeing samples it changes accuracy by at most \niAposMaxDAcc{} points on any tier, while ECE
rises on val, test and far (roughly doubling on val and test): there it adds confidence and nothing else, as
Lemma~\ref{lem:agree} predicts. The whole accuracy gain of the positive cache comes from the disagreeing samples, and the
gate keeps it. On near sets it instead lowers ECE slightly, because CLIP is slightly underconfident there. The negative cache improves both
metrics on the design tier, and keeping it adds accuracy over the gated positive cache alone on every tier.

\section{Limits and future work}\label{sec:limits}
\textbf{Where it helps} (Fig.~\ref{fig:where}). On agreeing samples the echo only adds confidence, so removing it should
help when CLIP is calibrated or overconfident and hurt when CLIP is underconfident. Across all \rhoN{} datasets the gate's
ECE change indeed follows CLIP's own confidence$-$accuracy gap (Spearman $\rho = \rhoGapAll$): the more overconfident
CLIP is, the more the gate lowers ECE. It holds within the design and far tiers too ($\rho = \rhoGapTest$ and \rhoGapFar). It is a tendency, not a
rule: GTSRB and Food101 lose a little although CLIP is not underconfident there, and Cars gains although it mildly
is. The gap needs labels, so it explains the
gate's behaviour but cannot drive it; label-free proxies are weaker (Appendix~\ref{app:switch}). We report the gate
always on, with this as its stated condition.

\textbf{Is there information beyond one bit of agreement?} The gate uses one bit: does the cache's top class agree with
CLIP's. Could a finer, still label-free, use of the scores do better? The accuracy gain comes from \emph{flips}, samples
where TDA's decision differs from CLIP's; a flip is right if TDA is right and wrong if CLIP was. We score ten label-free
per-sample quantities: three of CLIP's scores (its confidence, its top-1 $-$ top-2 margin, its probability of the
cache's class), three of the cache's (its confidence, its total mass, its weight on CLIP's class) and four of their
relation (their correlation over classes, their Jensen--Shannon similarity, the gap between their weights, and the
$\alpha$ at which the cache would flip CLIP's decision). The test is direct (Fig.~\ref{fig:noinfo}): for each quantity
we choose, on the val flips, the threshold and the side (block flips below or above it) that most raise accuracy when the
blocked flips are reverted to CLIP's decision, and apply that rule unchanged to every tier. An oracle that blocks exactly
the wrong flips would add \niOracleBlockLo{} to \niOracleBlockHi{} points per held-out tier, so there is room to gain.
No single-quantity rule takes it: even on val, where it is chosen, the best rule of each quantity loses accuracy (at
best \niRuleBestVal{} points), because right flips outnumber wrong ones in almost every range of every quantity, on every tier
(Fig.~\ref{fig:rates}). What sets the share of right flips is mostly the tier, not where a flip falls on any of these
scores. On the held-out tiers no rule gains more
than \niRuleBestHeld{} points. Combining all ten in a logistic regression fitted on the val flips does not help either:
it gains \niFilterVal{} points on val and loses \niFilterMax{} on far. Equivalently, in terms of the area under the ROC
curve (AUC; the probability that a random right flip scores higher than a random wrong one, 0.5 meaning no information),
the combined score reaches \niCombAucVal{} on val but \niCombAucNear{} on near and \niCombAucFar{} on far: it learns which datasets flip well, not which flips are
right. The scores of CLIP, of the cache and of their relation carry almost no information about when a flip is right;
within agreement-based control of the cache, the binary gate is close to the limit (details in
Appendix~\ref{app:switch}).

\textbf{Limitations.} (i) \emph{Underconfident CLIP.} Where CLIP is underconfident (Pets, Caltech101, ImageNet-R,
and among the other held-out sets CIFAR-10/100 and STL-10) the echo happens to correct it and the gate is worse than TDA in ECE, though it stays on the safer, underconfident side;
GTSRB and Food101 also lose slightly without that explanation. An exploratory switch that keeps TDA's echo on confident
streams recovers some of these, but was found after held-out results were known (Appendix~\ref{app:switch}). (ii) Where the gate is open, predictions are
still overconfident on the hardest sets (e.g.\ DTD); ZAEC on top helps on the test and far sets but not on near. Where CLIP is at or below chance (CLEVR-count, DMLab,
Retinopathy; Appendix~\ref{app:perds}), the gate restores calibration but neither TDA nor the gate recovers accuracy. (iii) One backbone (ViT-B/16) and one cache method; the echo argument applies to any cache
filled with the model's own pseudo-labels (e.g.\ DMN), which we did not test. (iv) The gate was chosen on the val splits of
the design tier after earlier attempts, some explored on test (Appendix~\ref{app:earlier}); the near and far tiers are the
clean evidence. (v) The near tier is small (three sets), and the tiers were revised once after a first look at the
results (\S\ref{sec:results}).

\section{Conclusion}
TDA's positive cache echoes CLIP: where it agrees with CLIP it cannot change the decision and only adds confidence.
Gating it on disagreement keeps TDA's accuracy everywhere and brings ECE back to CLIP's level on the design tier and on
datasets far from CLIP's training data, where TDA's calibration loss is largest. On data close to CLIP's training images TDA changes little and the gate changes little; per
dataset its effect depends on CLIP's own calibration: removing the echo is right when CLIP is not underconfident. Agreement is useful as one bit, and the label-free scores of CLIP and the cache say almost nothing more;
detecting underconfidence without labels is the open problem.

\section{Proofs}\label{app:proofs}
\textbf{Lemma 1.} Proved in the main text; $b_{\hat y} - b_j > 0$ holds strictly for a unique $\arg\max$.

\textbf{A no-flip bound.} Write $b = z - n$. Since $0 \le c_k \le S$, if
$z_{\hat y_0} - z_j > \alpha S + \max_k n_k - \min_k n_k$ for all $j \neq \hat y_0$, then
$b_{\hat y_0} - b_j > \alpha S$ and $o_{\hat y_0} - o_j > \alpha S - \alpha S = 0$: no cache content can change the
decision. We call such samples the \emph{no-flip region}.

\textbf{The self-vote bound.} If $x$ was admitted under $\hat y_0$ before the read, $c_{\hat y_0}(x) \ge e^{0} = 1$, and each
of the other at most $S$ terms of any class is at most $1$. A disagreement needs another class with kernel mass above
$c_{\hat y_0}(x) \ge 1$, i.e.\ at least two close neighbours of one other class.

\section{Exploratory: label-free proxies, a confidence switch, other uses of agreement}\label{app:switch}
\textbf{Label-free proxies of CLIP's calibration.} On the first \signalHead{} stream samples, the gate's ECE change follows
label-free proxies more weakly than CLIP's gap ($\rho = \rhoConfAll$ for CLIP's mean confidence, \rhoOverAll{} for the
share of samples with confidence above \confCut, \rhoShareAll{} for the share in the no-flip region of
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). Confidence is not calibration: CIFAR-100 (in between) is unconfident \emph{and} underconfident.

\textbf{A confidence switch.} On the design sets CLIP is underconfident exactly on the confident ones, so keeping TDA's
echo while the stream's running mean CLIP confidence exceeds \swThresh{} (gating otherwise) targets the gate's main failure
mode. It lowers mean ECE against the gate on test (\swGateTestEce{} $\to$ \swSwTestEce) and near (\swGateNearEce{} $\to$
\swSwNearEce; TDA: \swTdaNearEce) with accuracy unchanged, but the gain comes from the few confident sets it switches on
for, so we do not adopt it as the method. On far the switch stays off and equals the gate.

\textbf{Rule.} Keep TDA's logits while the running mean of CLIP's max-softmax confidence over the samples seen so far
exceeds a threshold; use the gate otherwise. It is online and label-free. \textbf{Selection.} The threshold is chosen on
the val splits of the five core sets and the test splits of Caltech101, UCF101 and Food101 (``dev'',
$^\ast$ in Table~\ref{tab:switch}), which contain
both kinds of stream: three confident sets on which CLIP is underconfident and five unconfident ones on which it is
overconfident (Table~\ref{tab:switchdesign}). It is the centre of the plateau of thresholds within 0.1 ECE of the minimum
on these sets, [\swPlateauLo, \swPlateauHi], giving \swThresh. No selection set has a mean confidence inside the
plateau, so any value in it gives the same result there: the threshold is underdetermined rather than fitted. The test
splits and the far sets are held out; Caltech101 and Food101 are in both the selection sets and the near tier, so near is
not clean evidence for the switch. The near tier alone would have preferred \swNearBestLo--\swNearBestHi{} (ECE
\swNearBestEce).

\textbf{Reading.} The switch is effectively a dataset-level choice: it is on for almost the whole stream of the
confident sets (final mean confidence $\ge$ \swMinOnConf) and off elsewhere ($\le$ \swMaxOffConf). Those sets carry the
whole gain; the others are unchanged.
\textbf{Caveat:} the idea came after held-out results were known, and a similar dataset-level switch had been dropped
before, so the held-out tiers are not clean evidence for it; it needs new datasets to be confirmed.

\textbf{What did not help} (exploratory, parameters chosen on val, checked on test and the held-out sets then available): $\alpha$ graded by the
strength of disagreement (it reduces to a rescaled constant $\alpha$); larger $\alpha$ (accuracy peaks at $\alpha \approx 2$--3
and the samples where the gate is open grow more overconfident); softening those samples by the CLIP--cache conflict (helps
on test, hurts on held-out sets); the cache--CLIP agreement rate as a label-free accuracy estimate (worse than CLIP's own
mean confidence, since the cache votes with CLIP's pseudo-labels); flip rules on disagreeing samples in terms of the
$\alpha$ needed to flip and CLIP's confidence (no held-out accuracy gain, although picking the right one of CLIP's and the
cache's top class per sample would add \niOraclePickLo--\niOraclePickHi{} points); and $\alpha$ scaled by the per-sample
correlation between the cache and CLIP logits over classes, including a bump that peaks at intermediate correlation and
vanishes at both ends (no held-out accuracy gain; the bump's val gain reverses on test and held-out sets, and the variants that
lower ECE do so by flipping fewer samples). These failures follow from \S\ref{sec:limits}: Fig.~\ref{fig:rates} shows
the share of right flips along each label-free quantity. Where a curve slopes on val and test it is flat or reversed on
near and far, and the level of each curve is set mostly by the tier. The flip filter of
\S\ref{sec:limits} blocks a flip when the regression's probability of a right flip is below \niThresh{} (chosen on val).
